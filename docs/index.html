01 / ABSTRACTResearch summary · Problem, approach & findings

Abstract

Understanding fluorescence microscopy images is fundamental to automating biological research. However, existing methods typically learn predefined concepts from closed-set data, limiting transfer across tasks and biological contexts. In this work, we introduce MicroCLIP, a vision–language model that learns generalizable cellular and subcellular representations from natural language supervision for open-set image classification and retrieval.

To pretrain MicroCLIP, we construct MicroPool-2M, a dataset of approximately 2.5 million fluorescence microscopy image–text pairs spanning diverse cellular structures, cell lines, and imaging conditions. Aligning microscopy images with biological concepts in natural language is challenging because most images lack descriptive captions and biological knowledge annotations. We therefore progressively establish image–text associations at three levels to provide MicroCLIP with richer semantic supervision: (1) category-level alignment links images to standardized labels defined by subcellular structure–cell-line combinations; (2) knowledge-level alignment incorporates biological priors derived from these labels to capture relationships across species and cell types; and (3) detail-level alignment uses a multimodal large language model to recaption and verify image–text pairs at scale, emphasizing fine-grained visual characteristics.

Extensive experiments demonstrate that MicroCLIP achieves leading performance in zero-shot classification and text–image retrieval on microscopy images. It retrieves images with specific cellular characteristics from thousands of candidates and generalizes to unseen biological concepts without additional supervision. These results highlight MicroCLIP’s potential as a foundation model for fluorescence microscopy. Models, training code, and datasets will be publicly released.

03 / OBSERVATIONThe biological grounding gap

Describing biology.
Identifying the image.

Biological knowledge and visual descriptions do not guarantee correct biological identification.

GPT-6 describes the biological properties of cytosol and SiHa cells and the diffuse, granular fluorescence visible in the microscopy image. However, in this example, it fails to connect those visual patterns to the correct subcellular structure and cell line: it predicts nucleoplasm in U-2 OS cells for an image annotated as cytosol in SiHa cells. Knowing the biological concepts and describing the image does not ensure that the model can match one to the other. This mismatch exposes a gap between biological semantics and microscopy images: the biological grounding gap.

On these diagnostic subsets, GPT-6 achieves 40.00% (12/30) on IID and 33.33% (10/30) on Compositional Shift; MicroCLIP achieves 100.00% (30/30) and 90.00% (27/30), respectively, using the same candidates and images.

Full knowledge annotation

Actin filaments are cytoskeletal polymers associated with the cell cortex and cytoplasm and are generally involved in mechanical support, cell motility, adhesion, intracellular transport, and cytokinesis. Cytosol is the aqueous component of the cytoplasm outside membrane-bound organelles and is generally involved in metabolism, signal transduction, and protein synthesis. U251MG cells originate from human brain tumor tissue, belong to the glial astrocytic lineage, and are classified as a malignant glioblastoma cell line; they are generally characterized by glial properties, a mutant TP53 background, and sustained proliferative capacity.

Full detail annotation

This monochrome fluorescence micrograph illustrates the Actin_filaments_and_Cytosol distribution within a clustered population of U-251_MG cells. The image is characterized by a dark, low-noise background that provides high contrast for the biological structures, which are arranged in a dense, overlapping formation occupying the majority of the field of view. The fluorescent signal manifests as a pervasive, granular texture throughout the cytoplasmic regions, creating a stippled appearance rather than distinct, continuous filamentous bundles. The cellular boundaries are largely defined by the termination of this speckled intensity, resulting in a cohesive, reticulated mass where individual cell margins are partially obscured by the density of the culture. Several discrete, hyper-intense punctate spots are visible within the brighter cellular regions, standing out against the more diffuse, mid-gray signal of the surrounding cytosol. The overall morphology suggests a confluent monolayer or aggregate where the cytoskeletal and cytoplasmic components are distributed extensively across the visible cell bodies.

This monochrome fluorescence micrograph illustrates the mitochondria distribution within a dense population of A549 cells. The image is characterized by a high-contrast signal against a uniformly dark background, highlighting specific subcellular regions while leaving the interstitial spaces largely devoid of fluorescence. The field of view reveals a confluent arrangement of cells where individual boundaries are not sharply defined, suggesting a tightly packed monolayer. Within the cellular clusters, the fluorescence signal manifests as a complex, reticular network of varying intensity, appearing as granular aggregates and interconnected strands rather than distinct, isolated organelles. This texture suggests a widespread cytoplasmic distribution of the targeted structures. The signal intensity is heterogeneous across the field, with certain regions displaying brighter, more concentrated accumulations of fluorescence, while others exhibit a fainter, more diffuse pattern. There is no evidence of distinct nuclear exclusion or specific perinuclear localization visible at this magnification, as the signal appears to permeate the available cytoplasmic volume of the cell clusters.
